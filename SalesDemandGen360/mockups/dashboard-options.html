What this buys you

  • The daily screen stays short. Four tiles and a list — a rep can clear it and leave.
  • Each screen has one job, so new metrics have an obvious home instead of a judgement call.
  • Different cadence of use. Dashboard is opened hourly; Pipeline weekly. Mixing them means the weekly stuff clutters the hourly view.
  • Filters make sense where they are — date ranges belong to retrospective analysis, not to "what needs me now".

What it costs

  • Two places to look for a number until the rule becomes second nature.
  • Some charts move off the dashboard you already reviewed — reply-rate-by-touch and persona performance shift to Pipeline.
  • A rep who wants "how are we doing" while looking at today has to switch screens.

One screen, three tabs. Pipeline disappears from the nav; everything numeric lives under Dashboard, separated by tab rather than by screen.

What this buys you

  • One nav item for numbers. Nobody has to remember which screen a metric lives on.
  • Zero duplication by construction — there is only one place a chart can go.
  • Shorter sidebar, which matters as Account Lists / Queues / Outreach already make it long.
  • Everything you reviewed on the current dashboard survives; it just moves behind a tab.

What it costs

  • Retrospective analysis gets buried. Tabs past the first are opened far less — the signal and persona data that should drive Learning is the stuff nobody clicks.
  • Two audiences, one screen. A rep wants Today; a manager wants Quality. Tabs make them share a home that suits neither.
  • Filters get ambiguous — a date range means something on Performance and nothing on Today, but it sits in the same header.
My recommendation: Option A. The two screens answer questions with different lifespans — "what do I do now" versus "is this working" — and they are read at different frequencies by different people. The cost of A is that a couple of charts move off the dashboard you already signed off on. The cost of B is that the retrospective data quietly stops being looked at, which is the data the Learning layer depends on to ever prove itself.
