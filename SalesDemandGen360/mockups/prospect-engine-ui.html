The most important constraint in the build Content is frozen when the lead is enrolled

All six attributes are written before the campaign starts, in one batch. LGM's own guidance is to fill the attributes before the campaign runs, and a timed rewrite is not safely possible: LGM exposes neither the scheduled send time per step nor whether a step reads its attribute at step start or at send. An update endpoint appears to exist, but writing into an unknown window with no way to confirm it landed is not something to build a send path on.

So once a campaign is in motion, the words cannot change. A new signal, a manual note, a rebuilt strategy — none of it reaches a sequence already running. The only reliable in-flight control is stop: pause or remove the lead so the remaining touches never fire.

What follows from that

Two rules, both enforced at generation rather than left to chance.

Early touches anchor to signals; late touches must not
Touch 6 is written on day 0 and sends on day 15. "You have 12 open roles right now" may be false by then. Late touches use durable framing — capability, proof points, a soft close — so they cannot go stale. Enforced in the drafting prompt, checked by evaluation.
An expiring signal stops the sequence rather than rewriting it
If a signal cited in an unsent touch expires, the remaining touches are stopped and the thread returns to you. Sending known-stale copy is worse than sending nothing.

The claim, stated precisely

Accounts carrying both a funding signal and a hiring signal within 90 days of each other booked a meeting at 21%, against a baseline of 9% for accounts with either signal alone. Measured across 45 accounts that reached outreach between 2026-06-01 and 2026-09-01.

Confounders worth checking before you promote. 29 of the 34 supporting cases are saas archetype, and 26 ran the same 6-touch cadence. This may be a SaaS effect or a cadence effect rather than a signal effect — the data cannot yet separate them. Promoting it as a global signal weight would apply a SaaS-derived finding to enterprise and e-commerce accounts.

Registered LGM cadences

Cadences are built and edited in La Growth Machine. Gen360 only records what exists there, so it knows which custom_attribute_N each step reads. Touch count and timing are LGM's to decide — Gen360 adopts them.

Drift is the failure mode to watch. If a sequence is edited in LGM and the registration here is not updated, Gen360 writes copy into the wrong slots — touch 3's message lands in touch 4's step, and neither system raises an error. LGM's API cannot read sequence structure, so step-level accuracy can only be confirmed by a person. What it can read turns that from a blind timer into a change detector.
